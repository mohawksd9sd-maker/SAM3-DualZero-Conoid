We construct a geometric Theory of Everything based on a \emph{Dual-Zero Hyperreal Spectral Triple} living on the Right Conoid and equipped with icosahedral symmetry. \end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The SAM3-DualZero-Conoid framework aims to realize a noncommutative geometric unification in which:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item The base classical geometry is a \textbf{right conoid}.
    \item Analytic control is provided by a custom \textbf{Dual-Zero hyperreal} extension.
    \item Discrete internal symmetry is introduced via \textbf{binary-icosahedral bridges}.
\end{itemize}

These structures are combined into a spectral triple. The spectral action principle is then expected to reproduce the Einstein--Hilbert action together with Standard Model gauge fields, Higgs sector, and fermions.

This note fixes the core definitions. Subsequent papers will construct the explicit Dirac operator, perform the spectral action expansion, and address renormalization and phenomenology.

\section{Right Conoid Geometry}

\subsection{Definition}

\begin{definition}[Right Conoid]
Let \( M \) be a ruled surface embedded in \( \mathbb{R}^3 \). We call \( M \) a \textbf{right conoid} if there exists a straight line \( \ell \) (the \textbf{axis}) such that every ruling of \( M \) intersects \( \ell \) orthogonally.
\end{definition}

We work throughout with the \textbf{infinite right conoid}, globally parametrized by
\[
\mathbf{r}: \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^3, \qquad
\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \bigl( v \cos u,\ v \sin u,\ h(u) \bigr),
\]
where \( u \in \mathbb{R} \) is the angular coordinate, \( v \in \mathbb{R} \) parametrizes position along each ruling, and \( h \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}) \) is a smooth profile function. The resulting surface is non-compact.

Choose the axis to be the \(z\)-axis. A standard parametrization is
\[
\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \bigl( v \cos u,\ v \sin u,\ h(u) \bigr),
\]
where \(u\) is the angular coordinate, \(v \in \mathbb{R}\) runs along each ruling, and \(h\) is a smooth function giving the height profile.

\begin{remark}
The choice \(h(u) = c u\) yields the \textbf{right helicoid} (a minimal ruled surface). Other choices recover Pl\"ucker's conoid and related Catalan surfaces.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Induced Riemannian Metric}

The partial derivatives are
\[
\mathbf{r}_u = (-v \sin u,\ v \cos u,\ h'(u)), \qquad
\mathbf{r}_v = (\cos u,\ \sin u,\ 0).
\]

The coefficients of the first fundamental form are
\[
E = v^2 + (h'(u))^2, \quad F = 0, \quad G = 1.
\]

Thus the induced metric on the infinite right conoid is
\begin{equation}
ds^2 = \bigl( v^2 + (h'(u))^2 \bigr)\, du^2 + dv^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Motivation for the Right Conoid}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Natural \(S^1\) rotational symmetry about the axis.
    \item Explicit ruled parametrization simplifies construction of the Clifford bundle and Dirac operator.
    \item The helicoid case is minimal, providing a geometrically canonical starting point.
    \item The ruling foliation offers a natural direction for noncommutative deformations and bridge actions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Recommended Working Model: The Right Helicoid}

For the initial construction of the spectral triple we adopt the profile function
\[
h(u) = c\, u, \qquad c > 0.
\]

This choice yields the \textbf{right helicoid}. It is a minimal surface (\( H = 0 \)) with negative Gaussian curvature and admits particularly clean explicit computations for the Dirac operator and spectral invariants.

\begin{remark}
We lock the right helicoid as the primary working example unless a compelling reason arises to retain a general profile function \( h(u) \) at this foundational stage.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Curvature of the Right Helicoid}

For the right helicoid with \( h(u) = c u \), we have \( h'(u) = c \), so the metric simplifies to
\begin{equation}
ds^2 = (v^2 + c^2)\, du^2 + dv^2.
\end{equation}

The Gaussian curvature is
\begin{equation}
K = -\frac{c^2}{(v^2 + c^2)^2} < 0.
\end{equation}

The mean curvature vanishes (\( H = 0 \)), confirming that the right helicoid is a minimal surface.

\subsection{Orthonormal Coframe and Volume Form}

An orthonormal coframe for the right helicoid is given by
\[
e^1 = \sqrt{v^2 + c^2}\, du, \qquad e^2 = dv.
\]

The induced volume form is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{vol} = \sqrt{v^2 + c^2}\, du \wedge dv.
\end{equation}

The area element along a ruling (fixed \( u \)) grows linearly with \( |v| \). This growth has important consequences for the spectrum of the Dirac operator and will require careful domain considerations or suitable cutoffs/weights at large \( |v| \).

\subsection{Dirac Operator on the Helicoid}

Using the orthonormal coframe above and a chosen spin connection, the Dirac operator on the right helicoid takes the schematic form
\[
D = i \gamma^1 \left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{v^2 + c^2}} \partial_u + \cdots \right) + i \gamma^2 \partial_v + \text{connection terms}.
\]

A full explicit derivation of the spin connection and the complete expression for \( D \) will be given in a later section.

\begin{remark}
Because the surface is non-compact and the rulings extend to infinity with linearly growing circumference, the spectrum of the (unbounded) Dirac operator is continuous rather than discrete. This necessitates the Dual-Zero hyperreal regularization introduced later to recover a well-defined spectral action and finite integrals.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Spin Structure}

The infinite right helicoid is an orientable surface and therefore admits spin structures. There exist two inequivalent spin structures. A definite choice (or a controlled way of working with both) will be required when constructing the spinor bundle and the geometric Dirac operator, especially once the binary-icosahedral bridges are introduced.

\subsection{Decisions to Lock for This Section}

The following points should be settled before proceeding to the Dual-Zero hyperreal structure:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Working surface.} Lock the right helicoid (\( h(u) = c u \)) as the main model for the foundational development?
    \item \textbf{Compactness.} Work directly with the infinite non-compact right conoid (as stated in the current repository vision), or introduce a compactification/cutoff at a later stage?
    \item \textbf{Dimension of the base.} Treat the conoid as a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold for the spectral triple, or plan an early dimensional lift (e.g., product constructions)?
    \item \textbf{Spin structure.} Keep both spin structures available for now, or fix one preferred spin structure at this stage?
\end{enumerate}

These decisions will determine the concrete analytic setting in which the Dual-Zero hyperreal structure and the binary-icosahedral bridges are subsequently defined.

\section{Dual-Zero Hyperreal Structure}

\begin{definition}[Dual-Zero Hyperreal Extension]
Let \(*\mathbb{R}\) be a hyperreal extension of \(\mathbb{R}\). A \textbf{Dual-Zero structure} consists of a distinguished pair of signed infinitesimals \(\varepsilon, \delta\) (or two monads around zero) equipped with a duality operation \(*\) satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \(\varepsilon \approx 0\), \(\delta \approx 0\), with \(\varepsilon \neq 0\), \(\delta \neq 0\);
    \item There is a canonical duality involution such that \(\varepsilon^* = \delta\) (or a symmetric controlled relation);
    \item The pair generates an infinitesimal ideal compatible with the field operations and order structure of \(*\mathbb{R}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

This structure refines classical nonstandard analysis by treating zero ``dually''. It enables symmetric infinitesimal corrections that survive in the finite part of the spectral action and supports exact transfer-principle arguments while maintaining regularization control.

\begin{remark}
In previous iterations of the Pure DYKES / SAM models, the Dual-Zero layer has been responsible for generating discrete structures (possible particle generations or chirality) through signed infinitesimal mechanisms.
\end{remark}

\section{Binary-Icosahedral Bridges}

\begin{definition}[Binary Icosahedral Group]
The \textbf{binary icosahedral group} \(2I\) (also written \(\langle 2,3,5 \rangle\)) is the unique perfect central extension
\[
1 \to \{\pm 1\} \to 2I \to A_5 \to 1
\]
of order 120. It embeds as a discrete subgroup of the unit quaternions \(\mathrm{Sp}(1) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2) \cong \mathrm{Spin}(3)\) and possesses a faithful 2-dimensional irreducible representation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Binary-Icosahedral Bridge]
A \textbf{binary-icosahedral bridge} is any structure morphism (representation, algebra homomorphism, or equivariant correspondence) that incorporates the representation theory or group action of \(2I\) into the spectral triple data on the Right Conoid.

Common realizations include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Equivariant action of \(2I\) on the spinor bundle of the conoid.
    \item Tensoring the algebra \(A\) with the group algebra \(\mathbb{C}[2I]\) (or its Dual-Zero hyperreal version).
    \item Using the finite spectral triple of the regular representation (or selected irreps) of \(2I\) as the internal finite geometry that bridges continuous geometry to Standard-Model-like degrees of freedom.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

These bridges are the mechanism through which icosahedral symmetry constrains the fluctuations of \(D\) and generates internal gauge and fermionic structure.

\section{Interconnection: The Dual-Zero Hyperreal Spectral Triple}

The three structures combine into a spectral triple of almost-commutative type:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The \textbf{Right Conoid} supplies the base Riemannian geometry whose Dirac operator generates the gravitational sector.
    \item The \textbf{Dual-Zero hyperreal} layer furnishes the analytic foundation on which functions, operators, and the cutoff are defined.
    \item The \textbf{binary-icosahedral bridges} supply discrete symmetry and internal degrees of freedom.
\end{itemize}

A schematic form of the spectral triple is
\[
A \;\simeq\; C^\infty_{\text{Dual-Zero}}(M) \;\hat{\otimes}\; \mathcal{A}_F^{2I},
\]
with Dirac operator
\[
D = D_{\text{conoid}} \otimes 1 + \gamma_5 \otimes D_F + \text{bridge terms} + \text{Dual-Zero corrections}.
\]

The spectral action \(\operatorname{Tr} f(D/\Lambda)\) is then expected to contain the Einstein--Hilbert term from the conoid together with Yang--Mills, Higgs, and fermionic terms generated by the bridges.
