# Neutrino Sector and PMNS — Hardened Status

## 1. Framework

- Type-I seesaw with right-handed Majorana masses generated by radial localization of RH modes on the conoid (and Dual-Zero suppression).
- Light-neutrino mixing from continuum defect overlaps (same geometric defect used in the quark sector) together with the charged-lepton diagonalizer.
- Dirac CP phase in the lepton sector arises once the complex structure of `A_F` is extended to the lepton embedding (parallel to the quark treatment).

## 2. Angles (representative hardened values)

θ₁₃ is the most robust geometric success in the lepton sector under the tip-defect construction. θ₁₂ and θ₂₃ remain sensitive to the precise RH mass hierarchy and to charged-lepton rotations.

## 3. Mass observables

| Observable | Status |
|------------|--------|
| Σ m_ν | Predictive in the seesaw + geometry; representative values O(0.06 eV) appeared in earlier model statements |
| m_ββ | Correlated with the light spectrum and Majorana phases; typically O(0.01–0.03 eV) in the geometric runs — allowed by current bounds |

These are **predictions**, not fits. They remain subject to the same continuum-localization caveat as the quark sector: pure kinetic zero modes are threshold-like; hierarchical RH masses use radial / Dual-Zero structure.

## 4. Majorana phases and RH spectrum

- Diagonal Majorana phases: linked to 2I characters / generation stabilizers (outlined).
- Off-diagonal structure: bridge overlaps of RH modes.
- RH mass hierarchy (1, r₂, r₃): from radial localization; applying a strong hierarchy without care can disturb the light-sector angles (residual tension identified in the hardening cycle).

## 5. Open issues

2. Full parallel of the quark `A_F` phase mechanism for δ_PMNS.
3. Boltzmann-level leptogenesis estimates (order-of-magnitude only so far).

## 6. Relation to the quark sector

The same continuum defect operator supplies the leading geometric mixing kernel for both quarks and leptons. The difference in final angles comes from:
- different radial localizations (charged leptons vs neutrinos vs quarks),
- the Type-I seesaw contraction,
- and the distinct `A_F` embeddings of lepton vs quark right-handed fields.
